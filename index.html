THINK IN THREE DIMENSIONS

The rules you already know, on a board you have to turn over in your hands. On a flat grid a cell touches up to 8 neighbours. Here it touches up to 26 — so the numbers run higher, and they mean more.

1

Rotate the cube

Drag to spin the board and look at it from any angle. A clue that makes no sense from the front often resolves the moment you see the side.

2

Reveal hidden cells

Tap a cell to open it. Safe ground clears outward on its own, exactly like the game you grew up with.

3

Analyse the clues

Each number counts the mines in all 26 cells around it — above, below, behind and diagonally through the cube.

4

Mark the mines

Flag the cells you have worked out, so the board keeps your reasoning while you go and solve the other side.

5

Explore internal layers

Pull the cube apart and look at the cells buried in the middle, then put it back together and keep going.

6

Clear every safe cell

The cube is solved when nothing safe is left hidden. Lives give you room to be wrong while you learn to see in three dimensions.

GO BEYOND THE SURFACE

A flat minesweeper has edges. A cube has an inside.

Most of this board is not on a face at all — it is buried in the middle, out of sight until you go looking. Open the layer view and the cube separates into slices you can read one at a time, so the cells nobody can see from outside stop being guesswork and start being solvable.

It is the one thing a flat grid cannot do, and on the larger cubes it is where most of the puzzle actually lives.

SIX WORLDS.
ONE CHALLENGE.

Same rules, six completely different places to think in. Each world starts dormant and comes back to life as you clear the board.

World 01

Classic — The Origin

Where it began. Clean cells on deep navy, nothing between you and the logic.

World 02

Wood — The Forgotten Workshop

Warm timber and lamplight. A puzzle box left on the bench, waiting to be picked back up.

World 03

Space — The Lost Station

Adrift past a dead planet. Cold light, slow debris, and a board that glows in the dark.

World 04

Nature — The Living Forest

Moss, blossom and fireflies in a sunlit clearing. The quietest board, and the one that grows back.

World 05

Minimal — The White Dimension

Everything stripped away. No scenery, no colour, no excuses — only the problem in front of you.

World 06

Iron — The Last Foundry

Steel over a furnace, gears still turning. Dark, heavy, and built for the biggest cubes.

START SMALL.
THINK BIG.

Four cube sizes. Every step up adds a layer in all three directions at once, so the board does not just get wider — it gets deeper, and far more of it ends up hidden inside.

3×3×3

27 cells, 1 hidden inside

4×4×4

64 cells, 8 hidden inside

5×5×5

125 cells, 27 hidden inside

6×6×6

216 cells, 64 hidden inside

Bigger cubes are a different problem, not a longer one

On a 3×3×3 a single cell is buried out of sight. On a 6×6×6 there are sixty-four of them, stacked four deep. The logic never changes — but holding the whole shape in your head does.
